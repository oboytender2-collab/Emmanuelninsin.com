MY STORY

From childhood dreams to a bigger stage.

My story is one of ambition, setbacks, mentorship, persistence, faith and continued growth. The journey is not over; every new chapter adds to the story of the person I am becoming.

01

Childhood & Basic School

My early education at Ntontom M/A Basic School and Praprababida M/A JHS helped build my ambition, discipline and determination to do well academically.

02

SHS: University Practice

I studied General Arts at University Practice Senior High School, taking Geography, Economics, CRS and Government. Despite financial challenges, I kept learning and benefited from the guidance of mentors including Sir Andy Amo Smith, known as Khaptain, and Francis Ogoe, known as Pay Money.

03

Lessons in Leadership

Serving as Grounds Prefect taught me about responsibility and the importance of fair, transparent procedures. That experience strengthened my interest in fairness, accountability and justice.

04

The Road to UCC

After SHS, I spent about two years at home and worked as a mobile money agent and Direct Sales Representative for Ramstel Telecom. An encouraging conversation with Mr Seba helped me consider UCC CODE and opened a new path back to education.

05

The Journey Continues at UCC CODE

I am pursuing a Bachelor of Education in Social Studies at the University of Cape Coast's College of Distance Education. The journey continues to challenge me academically and personally, while strengthening my commitment to learning and growth.

06

Leadership & Student Welfare

My commitment to service led me to contest for Central Regional Welfare Officer of DESAG-UCC for the 2026/2027 academic year. With a campaign focused on “Welfare First,” inclusion and communication, I was elected and sworn into office on 10 October 2026 at the UCC CODE Conference Centre. I see the position as a responsibility to listen, communicate and serve fellow students.

07

My Vision for Law & Justice

I aspire to study law in Ghana and become a lawyer who contributes to human rights, fair trials and better access to justice, especially for people who face barriers to legal support. My experiences have taught me that leadership and education matter most when they are guided by integrity and concern for others.

Senior High School: Growth Through Challenge

Returning to Cape Coast for SHS felt like coming home after leaving the city in 2008. I joined University Practice SHS as a General Arts student. Although Free SHS reduced some of the burden, my family still struggled to meet the cost of books and other school needs. My parents were low-income earners, and staying in touch with home could be difficult because of poor network coverage where I had lived.

Khaptain became one of the most influential people in my life, offering guidance and encouragement beyond academics. Pay Money also supported my development, and I was able to attend their vacation classes without paying the usual fees. My young uncle Paul Ninsin, known as Slym, helped with educational needs despite his own challenges. Their support taught me to value generosity, mentorship and the people who help others move forward.

My school years also brought friendships, disappointments and important lessons about trust and fairness. After being vetted and elected Grounds Prefect, I was later removed and replaced by someone who, in my view, had not gone through the same vetting process. The experience left me thinking about the need for clear rules and equal treatment. It became one of the experiences that shaped my interest in justice and accountable leadership.

From Work to UCC CODE

After SHS, I remained at home for approximately two years before finding a new direction. I worked as a mobile money agent and a Direct Sales Representative for Ramstel Telecom. During that period, I met Mr Seba while handling a transaction. He encouraged me to apply to UCC CODE, and his advice helped me see that my educational journey could continue even if it did not follow the path I had first imagined.

At UCC CODE, I began studying for a Bachelor of Education in Social Studies. Lecturers and fellow students have contributed to my development, and my studies have required persistence, adjustment and a willingness to seek help when needed. I continue to learn from both my successes and my difficulties.

Leadership: Welfare First

My election as DESAG-UCC Central Regional Welfare Officer for 2026/2027 is a milestone, but I regard it first as a duty to serve. My campaign emphasised student welfare, inclusion and communication. I want students to feel that their concerns can be heard and that leadership should be approachable, responsible and focused on practical solutions.

I know that holding office does not mean having every answer. It means listening carefully, working with others, seeking guidance and being accountable. My earlier school experience taught me how deeply fairness matters, and I want those lessons to inform the way I approach student leadership.

The Journey Is Not Over

My story continues. From basic school to SHS, from work after graduation to UCC CODE, and from early leadership experiences to student welfare service, every stage has helped shape my goals. I hope my journey encourages other students and young people who face delays, financial challenges or uncertainty to keep working towards their ambitions.

This is not the end of my story. It is another chapter in a journey of education, service, growth and purpose.
