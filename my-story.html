MY STORY

From childhood dreams to a bigger stage.

My story is one of ambition, setbacks, mentorship, persistence, faith and continued growth. The journey is not over; every new chapter adds to the story of the person I am becoming.

01

Childhood & Basic School

My educational journey began at Ntontom M/A Basic School before I continued to Praprababida M/A JHS. From an early age, I was determined to perform well and was often among the top two students in my class. I was motivated by my mother's record of coming first throughout her own basic education, and I wanted to build on that example.

At primary level, I received recognition for English reading in the Kyekyewere Circuit. Junior high school challenged me to become more disciplined and competitive. I completed the BECE with aggregate 12, which I regarded as a significant achievement and a record for my school at the time. These years helped shape my belief that persistence and education can open doors.

02

SHS & Leadership

I attended University Practice Senior High School in Cape Coast, studying General Arts with Geography, Economics, Christian Religious Studies and Government. Although Free SHS helped with tuition, meeting the cost of books and other school needs remained difficult for my family. That experience taught me to value opportunities and keep working even when circumstances were not easy.

Mentors such as Sir Andy Amo Smith, known as Khaptain, and Francis Ogoe, known as Pay Money, supported my learning and encouraged my growth. My uncle Paul Ninsin, known as Slym, also helped me with educational needs. At school, my experience as Grounds Prefect and the challenges surrounding that role taught me lessons about leadership, fairness and proper procedures. My friendships and experiences at SHS became important parts of the person I am today.

03

The Road to UCC

After completing SHS, I spent approximately two years at home while trying to find a way forward. The delay was difficult, but it did not end my ambition to continue my education. During that period, I gained work experience as a mobile money agent and a Direct Sales Representative (DSR) for Ramstel Telecom. Working taught me responsibility, patience and how to relate to people from different backgrounds.

A turning point came when I met Mr. Seba during a transaction. He encouraged me to consider the University of Cape Coast's College of Distance Education (UCC CODE). His advice helped me see a practical route back into education. Entering UCC CODE became an important milestone: a reminder that progress may take time, and that an unexpected conversation can help open a new door.

04

The Journey Continues

I am continuing my Bachelor of Education (B.Ed.) in Social Studies at UCC CODE. University life has challenged me through assignments, group work, examinations and demanding courses, while also helping me grow academically and personally. Sociology, Economics, Geography, Government and educational foundations have contributed to how I understand people, institutions and society. My teaching observation and off-campus teaching experience at University Practice SHS have also strengthened my interest in education.

In 2026, I was elected DESAG-UCC Central Regional Welfare Officer for the 2026/2027 academic year and was sworn into office on 10 October 2026 at the UCC CODE Conference Centre. I see this responsibility as an opportunity to listen to students, communicate their concerns and contribute to their welfare. Alongside my studies and leadership, I am preparing for my long-term ambition to study law and advocate for human rights, fair trials, equal treatment and access to justice for less privileged people. My story remains in progress, and I intend to keep learning, serving and growing.
